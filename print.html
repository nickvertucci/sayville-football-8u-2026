Regular I Sneak Right

Regular I Z Right 10 SneakRUNQB

Fourth and inches. The quarterback keeps it behind the center's playside hip before the defense is out of its stance. No handoff to go wrong and no back to get tackled in the backfield.

Assignments vs 5-3

LTE
Shoulder to shoulder with the man beside you, and push. Low pads — never look for a man.
LT

LG

C
Shoulder to shoulder with the man beside you, and push. Low pads — never look for a man. One yard is all we need, and it comes from you.
RG
Shoulder to shoulder with the man beside you, and push. Low pads — never look for a man.
RT
Shoulder to shoulder with the man beside you, and push. Low pads — never look for a man.
RTE
Shoulder to shoulder with the man beside you, and push. Low pads — never look for a man.
Z
Release downfield and run the corner off. You will not get the ball, just his eyes.
QB
Go on the snap behind the guard's outside hip on the Z side. Low pads, ball tight, fall forward.
FB
Shoulder to shoulder with the man beside you, and push. Low pads — never look for a man. Both hands on the man in front of you, and push.
TB
Shoulder to shoulder with the man beside you, and push. Low pads — never look for a man. Push the pile. If the ball comes loose, you are closest to it.

Coaching points

  • This is a one-yard play. Do not call it needing two.
  • The snap count is the whole play. If they know it is coming they still cannot stop it, but only if we go on the first sound and they do not.
  • Expect the heaviest front the league allows on fourth and one. Nobody blocks a man on this play, so it does not change the call.
  • Teach the quarterback to fall forward the moment he feels contact. Eight-year-olds try to spin and lose the yard they already had. And he does not get to pick a hole — there is no hole, there is a snap count and a yard.
  • It is legal for us in 11-man play. It is specifically prohibited in the 8-man Rookie division, so if we ever end up there this play comes straight out of the book.

Regular I Dive Right

Regular I Z Right 20 DiveRUNFB

The quickest handoff in the book. The fullback is through the line before their linebackers have read anything. We run it to remind them the middle is not free, which is what makes the toss work later.

Assignments vs 5-3

LTE
End head up on you. Cut him off — get between him and the ball.
LT
Tackle on your inside shoulder. Cut him off — get between him and the ball.
LG
Nobody on you. Cut off the backside. Never quit on the play.
C
Reach the nose to your right. Head across his playside shoulder.
RG
Nobody on you. Help on the nose, then take the middle linebacker.
RT
Block down on the tackle, inside shoulder. Head across him — nobody crosses your face.
RTE
End head up on you. Drive him to the sideline. The ball goes inside you.
Z
Run at the corner and screen him off. Stay in his way.
QB
Open a quarter turn, hand to the fullback at two yards, then fake outside.
FB
Aim at the center's outside hip, just off the double team. Get north, no dancing.
TB
Run the toss path outside and sell it. You will not get the ball.

Coaching points

  • Snap to handoff should be under a second. If the fullback is waiting on the ball, the play is already dead.
  • Best call when their linebackers start cheating wide to stop the toss.
  • The fullback runs at a spot, not at a defender. Give him the outside hip of the center and let him go.
  • Best short-yardage call in the book. Nobody has to read anything and the ball is over the line before the defense moves.

Regular I Z Right 30 WedgeRUNTB

Short yardage and the goal line. Nobody blocks a man — the whole line squeezes shoulder to shoulder on the center and pushes. The tailback puts a hand on the center's back and goes wherever the pile goes.

Assignments vs 5-3

LTE
Shoulder to shoulder with the man beside you, and push. Low pads — never look for a man.
LT
Shoulder to shoulder with the man beside you, and push. Low pads — never look for a man.
LG

C
Shoulder to shoulder with the man beside you, and push. Low pads — never look for a man. You are the tip of the wedge — everybody comes to you.
RG
Shoulder to shoulder with the man beside you, and push. Low pads — never look for a man.
RT
Shoulder to shoulder with the man beside you, and push. Low pads — never look for a man.
RTE
Shoulder to shoulder with the man beside you, and push. Low pads — never look for a man.
Z
Run at the corner and screen him off. Stay in his way. Nothing comes around the outside into the back of the wedge.
QB
Snap and hand to the tailback immediately, then stay out of his way. Never ride the handoff.
FB
Shoulder to shoulder with the man beside you, and push. Low pads — never look for a man. Both hands on the tailback's back and drive. You are pushing, not blocking.
TB
Hand on the center's back, pads low, follow the wedge. Never cut or bounce — the hole moves with you.

Coaching points

  • Two yards is a good wedge. Do not call it needing four.
  • Expect the heaviest front the league allows inside the five. It does not change the call — nobody is picking a man anyway.
  • Nothing in the league rulebook prohibits wedge blocking for 11-man play, but it is the first thing leagues take away. Re-check it every season before you install it.
  • Coach the shoulders, not the feet. If there is daylight between two linemen the wedge is a line of five men getting pushed backwards instead of one thing moving forward.
  • Low pads win this and nothing else does. The team that stands up loses.

Regular I Iso Right

Regular I Z Right 32 IsoRUNTB

The whole play is one block: the fullback isolates their linebacker in the hole and the tailback runs off whichever way that block goes. It teaches a back to read a block, which is the skill this formation is here to build.

Assignments vs 5-3

LTE
End head up on you. Cut him off — get between him and the ball.
LT
Tackle on your inside shoulder. Cut him off — get between him and the ball.
LG
Nobody on you. Cut off the backside. Never quit on the play.
C
Reach the nose to your right. Head across his playside shoulder.
RG
Nobody on you. Help on the nose, then take the middle linebacker.
RT
Block down on the tackle, inside shoulder. Head across him — nobody crosses your face.
RTE
End head up on you. Drive him to the sideline. The ball goes inside you.
Z
Run at the corner and screen him off. Stay in his way.
QB
Reverse pivot, hand to the tailback at four yards, then clear out and fake wide.
FB
Lead through the hole. Block the first man who shows in it. Move him any direction — just move him.
TB
Take the handoff at four yards and run downhill off the fullback's block. He goes outside, you cut inside.

Coaching points

  • Drill the fullback's block by itself with a bag. He does not have to win — he has to make the linebacker pick a side.
  • Teach the tailback one rule: cut off the fullback's back. Do not let him decide before he gets there.
  • If their linebacker is faster than our fullback, stop calling this and run I Slant instead.
  • The tailback should be running downhill at the hole, not sideways looking for one.

Regular I Power Right

Regular I Z Right 34 PowerRUNTB

Our best downhill run. Everybody blocks down inside, the Z kicks the end out, and the fullback leads through the hole in front of the tailback. Nobody leaves his spot — every block on the card is made by the kid already standing there.

Assignments vs 5-3

LTE

LT
Block down on the tackle, inside shoulder. Head across him — nobody crosses your face.
LG
Nobody on you. Cut off the backside. Never quit on the play.
C

RG
Nobody on you. Help on the nose, then take the middle linebacker.
RT
Block down on the tackle, inside shoulder. Head across him — nobody crosses your face.
RTE
Leave the end — he is kicked out. Go take the outside linebacker.
Z
Kick the end out. Aim at his outside hip. Never let him come underneath you.
QB
Open right, hand deep to the tailback, then fake the boot. It holds the backside end.
FB
Lead through the hole. Block the first man who shows in it.
TB
Take the handoff downhill at our tackle's outside hip, following the fullback. Never bounce it outside.

Coaching points

  • This is the most physical run in the book. If it works you can run it fifteen times, and at this age you often can.
  • Drill the Z's kick-out on its own. If he blocks the end with his shoulders square, the end slides underneath and makes the tackle for a loss — he has to attack the outside hip.
  • The fullback's man is whoever shows in the hole, not a man he picks before the snap. Tell him that every time.
  • The tailback's most common mistake is bouncing it wide looking for green grass. The yards are inside the Z's block. Make him run it tight in practice until it is automatic.

  • With nobody pulling, the tight end kicks the end out himself. Against a four-linebacker front that leaves the outside linebacker free — the Z takes him if he fills, and if he keeps making the tackle, run Toss at him instead.
