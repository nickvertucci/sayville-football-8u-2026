I Formation - Strong Right - Off tackle right

Regular I SL Right 34 PowerRUNTB

Our best downhill run. Everybody blocks down inside, the SL kicks the end out, and the fullback leads through the hole in front of the tailback. Nobody leaves his spot — every block on the card is made by the kid already standing there.

Assignments vs 5-3

LTE
End head up on you. Cut him off — get between him and the ball.
LT
Block down on the tackle, inside shoulder. Head across him — nobody crosses your face.
LG
Nobody on you. Cut off the backside. Never quit on the play.
C
Reach the nose to your right. Head across his playside shoulder.
RG
Nobody on you. Help on the nose, then take the middle linebacker.
RT
Block down on the tackle, inside shoulder. Head across him — nobody crosses your face.
RTE
Leave the end — he is kicked out. Go take the outside linebacker.
SL
Kick the end out. Aim at his outside hip. Never let him come underneath you.
QB
Open right, hand deep to the tailback, then fake the boot. It holds the backside end.
FB
Lead through the hole. Block the first man who shows in it.
TB
Take the handoff downhill at our tackle's outside hip, following the fullback. Never bounce it outside.

Coaching points

  • This is the most physical run in the book. If it works you can run it fifteen times, and at this age you often can.
  • Drill the SL's kick-out on its own. If he blocks the end with his shoulders square, the end slides underneath and makes the tackle for a loss — he has to attack the outside hip.
  • The fullback's man is whoever shows in the hole, not a man he picks before the snap. Tell him that every time.
  • The tailback's most common mistake is bouncing it wide looking for green grass. The yards are inside the SL's block. Make him run it tight in practice until it is automatic.

I Formation - Strong Right - LTE jet right

Regular I SL Right LTE JetRUNLTE

The left tight end comes all the way across at the snap on an end-around, takes the handoff at full speed and gets to the right edge before the defense can turn, while the tailback's fake the other way holds the linebackers.

Assignments vs 5-3

LTE
Stay down in your stance — no motion. At the snap run flat behind the quarterback, take the handoff at full speed and get to the right edge. Turn up outside the fullback.
LT
Tackle on your inside shoulder. Cut him off — get between him and the ball.
LG
Nobody on you. Cut off the backside. Never quit on the play.
C
Reach the nose to your right. Head across his playside shoulder.
RG
Nobody on you. Climb to the playside linebacker. Head across him.
RT
Block down on the tackle, inside shoulder. Head across him — nobody crosses your face.
RTE
End head up on you. Turn him inside. The ball goes around behind you.
SL
Run at the corner and screen him off. Stay in his way.
QB
Open right and hand the ball to the left tight end as he comes across behind you, then fake a run to the left.
FB
Lead outside our end, then turn up inside — the playside linebacker is the man who shows. Head across him.
TB
Take a fake handoff and run hard at the left side, off tackle, to pull the linebackers with you.

Coaching points

  • No motion before the snap. The end stays down in his stance, so the play is legal and nothing tips it off.
  • The handoff is the whole play: the quarterback opens right and puts the ball in the end's belly at full speed, then carries out his fake.
  • The tailback's fake to the left has to be hard. It is what keeps the backside linebackers from chasing the end down.

Split formation - Strong right - Quick pitch right

Split SL Right 28 PitchRUNLH

Get outside in a hurry. The far back trails the quarterback, takes the pitch on the run and turns up inside the block out on the edge. The split man makes that block — their corner, or their outside linebacker against the 5-4-2, which has no corners — and that is what frees both backs to stay in the backfield where the fake lives.

4-4

Base4 down4 LB3 DB

Four down linemen on the guards and the ends. Two inside linebackers and two outside linebackers at three yards, with the outside pair responsible for forcing everything back in. Two corners and a free safety behind. This is the front we play unless there is a reason not to.

Assignments

LE
Attack the outside shoulder of the man over you and keep your outside arm free. Squeeze down, but never let anything cross your face to the outside.
LT
Attack the inside shoulder of the guard and squeeze the middle. You and the other tackle keep the center from climbing to a linebacker.

Outside linebacker. You are the force man — turn everything back inside and never get hooked. If it is a pass, you have the flat.

Three yards off, outside the end. Nothing outside you and nothing behind you. Force it back in.
RC
Three yards off, outside the end. Nothing outside you and nothing behind you. Force it back in.
FS
Eight yards deep in the middle. Nobody gets behind you. Take the cut-off angle, not the chase angle.

Coaching points

  • This is our base, so it is the front the kids should be able to line up in without being told. Nearly everything that hurts you at this age gets outside, and two extra defenders who can run beats two extra defenders who can only push.
  • The two outside linebackers are the reason this front works. They force everything back inside — if either one gets hooked and the ball gets outside him, it is a long run every time.
  • We give up a body inside to get it, so an offense that runs straight at the middle will find room. If they start hammering the A gaps, call Tight and put the fifth man back on the line.
  • The two inside linebackers are stacked behind the guards, not over the center, so neither one owns the middle by himself. Whichever one the ball declares to fills it, and the other takes the cutback — say it every practice or they both chase.
  • Nobody crosses the line before the snap. The linebackers read one step, then go.

Why it is legal

Four down linemen, four linebackers at three yards, and three defensive backs no closer than three yards. Comfortably inside every limit. No blitzing.

5-3

Tight5 down3 LB3 DB

Coaching points

  • Call this when they are winning the middle. The Base 4-4 leaves the guards uncovered on purpose; the moment a team makes us pay for it, the nose tackle goes back in and this is the front.
  • Every down lineman has a gap and nothing else. The moment one of them chases the ball instead of holding his gap, we give up the play we were built to stop.
  • Contain is the whole job for the ends. A back who gets outside our end at this age usually scores — teach them to keep the outside arm free and squeeze everything in.
  • Linebackers read the back closest to them, not the ball. The ball lies to them; the blocking does not.
  • Nobody may cross the line before the snap. Blitzing is illegal at this age, so the linebackers wait, read one step, then attack downhill.

Why it is legal

Five down linemen (cap is six), three linebackers at three yards (minimum is three at two yards), and all three defensive backs at three yards or deeper (minimum is two). No blitzing.

6-3

Goal Line6 down3 LB2 DB

Six down linemen is the legal maximum. Linebackers sit at two yards, the closest they are allowed to be, and both corners come up to two yards. There is no safety in this front — everybody is in the box, which is the point.

Coaching points

  • Everybody plays low. Whoever gets under the other team's pads wins the down, and at this age that is nearly the whole game on the goal line.
  • This front has no safety. If they get past the linebackers there is nobody left, so do not call it outside the five unless it is short yardage.
  • Against a wedge, the middle linebacker meets it low and the two guards get penetration to break it up. A wedge that keeps moving forward scores.
  • Two yards is the closest the linebackers may legally line up. Do not let them creep, and do not let anyone lean forward before the snap — that is a blitz and it is a penalty on the head coach.

Why it is legal

Six down linemen, which is the cap. Three linebackers at two yards and both corners at two yards, which is the minimum depth. No blitzing.

6-2-3

Coaching points

  • This is not a call, it is a requirement. Rule 15.04 — the moment we go up by 18, after the extra point, this is the defense until the margin drops back under 18.
  • Our starting backfield, quarterback included, also has to come out on offense at the same time (15.03). On a roster of fifteen or more they leave the game — they do not slide to the line — and the board asks that we run it up the middle and not throw. Know all of that before it happens, not while an official is explaining it to you.
  • There are no mandatory substitutions on defense under this rule (15.07), so you can keep whoever is in.
  • Teach the kids why. We are up by three touchdowns against eight-year-olds — the job now is to be gracious, tackle cleanly and let the clock run.

Why it is legal

Mandated by rule 15.04, which overrides the usual minimum of three linebackers — this front is required to have exactly two, five yards off, with three safeties ten yards deep. Ends are seven yards outside the offensive tackles per 15.05.
