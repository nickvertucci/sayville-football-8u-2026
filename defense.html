Defensive playbook

Three fronts. One for most downs, one for when they have to have a yard, and one for when they keep getting outside us. Every alignment here is checked against the league rulebook by the generator — an illegal front fails the build.

The fronts

Base

5-3

Our everyday front. Five down linemen cover the offensive line, three linebackers fill, and a free safety cleans up anything that gets through.

5 down  ·  3 linebackers  ·  3 defensive backs

Goal Line

6-3

The heaviest front the rulebook allows. Six down linemen shoulder to shoulder and three linebackers right behind them, for third-and-one and inside the five.

6 down  ·  3 linebackers  ·  2 defensive backs

Wide

4-4

Four down linemen and four linebackers. Trades a body on the line for a fourth linebacker, so we have two extra defenders who can run to the edge.

4 down  ·  4 linebackers  ·  3 defensive backs

Prevent

6-2-3

Not a choice. The moment we lead by 18 the rulebook requires this exact front, with these exact depths, until the margin drops back under 18.

6 down  ·  2 linebackers  ·  3 defensive backs

What the rulebook allows

For 8- and 9-year-olds the league caps the defensive line at six, requires at least three linebackers no closer than two yards, and keeps defensive backs at two yards or deeper.

Blitzing is illegal at all times. No defender may move forward before the snap. Gap penetration after the snap is fine — that is not a blitz.

Circumventing this is a 15-yard unsportsmanlike penalty on the head coach, and a second offense gets him ejected. Full detail in the rules page.
