The 2026 playbook

Two offensive formations, 15 plays, three defensive fronts, built for 11-on-11 8U tackle. Every diagram is generated from the play files in the repo — so what is on this site is exactly what is in the binder.

Fastest way to find a play: the call sheet.

Formations, in teaching order

I-Formation

8 plays

Fullback and tailback stacked behind the quarterback. Teaches a back to read a block instead of running to a spot.

Calls start with I

Wishbone

7 plays

Three backs in a Y. Three threats every snap and a symmetric formation, so every play works both directions.

Calls start with Bone

Defensive playbook

Three fronts, each checked against the league rulebook by the generator: a base for most downs, a goal-line front for when they have to have a yard, and a wider front for when they keep getting outside us.

Base

5-3

Our everyday front. Five down linemen cover the offensive line, three linebackers fill, and a free safety cleans up anything that gets through.

Goal Line

6-3

The heaviest front the rulebook allows. Six down linemen shoulder to shoulder and three linebackers right behind them, for third-and-one and inside the five.

Wide

4-4

Four down linemen and four linebackers. Trades a body on the line for a fourth linebacker, so we have two extra defenders who can run to the edge.

How we call plays

Every play has a teaching name (Power Right) and a huddle call (I 6 Power). Both are printed on every card. The call is always formation + hole + play word, and the hole numbers are the only thing the kids have to memorize — odd to the left, even to the right, counting outward.

0
Straight up the middle
1 / 2
A gap — left / right of the center
3 / 4
B gap — between guard and tackle
5 / 6
Off tackle — outside the tackle
7 / 8
Outside — around the end

So Bone 8 Pitch is the wishbone, outside right, pitch, and I 4 Iso is the I-formation, right B gap, isolation — a kid who knows the system can run either one the first time he hears it.

Before you install anything

The league rulebook constrains this playbook in ways that matter: 8- and 9-year-olds face a minimum of three linebackers and no blitzing, which makes the 6-2 illegal, and 8-year-olds may be placed in an 8-man division where none of this is legal at all.
