The 2026 playbook

Four offensive formations, 48 plays, four defensive fronts, built for 11-on-11 8U tackle. Every diagram is generated from the play files in the repo — so what is on this site is exactly what is in the binder.

Fastest way to find a play: the call sheet.

Formations, in teaching order

Regular I

24 plays

Fullback and tailback stacked behind the quarterback. Teaches a back to read a block instead of running to a spot.

Calls start with I

Power I

6 plays

Three backs in the backfield: fullback and tailback stacked, and the wingback offset beside them. An extra runner and an extra blocker at the point of attack over the base I.

Calls start with Power

Wishbone

10 plays

Three backs in a Y. Three threats every snap and a symmetric formation, so every play works both directions.

Calls start with Bone

Full House

8 plays

Three backs in a straight line across, all at the same depth. Perfectly balanced, perfectly symmetric, and the defense cannot tell from the alignment which of the three is getting it.

Calls start with House

Defensive playbook

Four fronts, every one of them checked against the league rulebook by the generator: a base for most downs, a goal-line front for when they have to have a yard, a wider front for when they keep getting outside us, and the prevent the league requires at an 18-point lead.

Base

5-3

Our everyday front. Five down linemen cover the offensive line, three linebackers fill, and a free safety cleans up anything that gets through.

Goal Line

6-3

The heaviest front the rulebook allows. Six down linemen shoulder to shoulder and three linebackers right behind them, for third-and-one and inside the five.

Wide

4-4

Four down linemen and four linebackers. Trades a body on the line for a fourth linebacker, so we have two extra defenders who can run to the edge.

Prevent

6-2-3

Not a choice. The moment we lead by 18 the rulebook requires this exact front, with these exact depths, until the margin drops back under 18.

How we call plays

Every play has a teaching name (I Slant Right) and a huddle call (I Z Right 36 Slant). Both are printed on every card. In the Regular I the call is formation + flanker + back + hole + play word. Z Right is where the flanker lines up, then the first number says who carries it and the second says where it goes.

Who carries it

1
Quarterback — every formation
2
Fullback — every formation
3
Tailback in the Regular I and the Power I  ·  right halfback in the Wishbone and the Full House
4
Flanker in the Regular I  ·  wingback in the Power I  ·  left halfback in the Wishbone and the Full House

Where it goes — even numbers to the right (0, 2, 4, 6, 8), odd to the left (1, 3, 5, 7, 9), counting outward from the center. This is the only part the kids have to memorize.

0 / 1
Between the center and the guard
2 / 3
Between the guard and the tackle
4 / 5
Between the tackle and the end
6 / 7
Outside the tight end
8 / 9
Wider still — all the way outside

So I Z Right 20 Dive is flanker right, fullback, 0 hole, and I Z Right 36 Slant is flanker right, tailback, outside the tight end. A kid who knows the two numbers can run a play the first time he hears it.

Every play in the book is Z Right today. It is in the call anyway so a Z Left look can be added later without changing the language.

Every formation uses the same two digits — Power I 34 Power, Bone 20 Dive. Only the first word and the backs change, so a kid who learns the numbers once can call a play in any of them.

Before you install anything

The league rulebook constrains this playbook in ways that matter: 8- and 9-year-olds face a minimum of three linebackers and no blitzing, which makes the 6-2 illegal, and 8-year-olds may be placed in an 8-man division where none of this is legal at all.

Read the rulebook before you install anything.
